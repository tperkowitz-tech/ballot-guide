Ballot Guide Prompt Kit

Build a sourced guide to your ballot. Neutral comparison by default; optional scores against your own values.

Before you start

You will need: an AI chat that can search the web (for example Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity, ChatGPT or Claude), and about 10 to 20 minutes for a short ballot. This page writes the questions for you. You copy each question into the AI chat, then paste the AI's answer back here. The page keeps your progress in this browser only.

How it works

  1. Tell the page about you.
  2. Ask the AI to list your ballot.
  3. Ask the AI about each race and measure.
  4. Check a few sources and build your guide.
Words used here
Record
Something a candidate actually did (a vote, a ruling, an official act).
Funder
Who gave money to the campaign.
Statement
What a candidate says, such as a website or interview.
Measure
A yes/no question on the ballot, such as a proposition, an initiative, or a local tax (levy).
Uncontested
Only one candidate is running.
Score
A summary of the evidence found and how each piece was marked as for or against your priorities. It is not a prediction of who will win.
Coverage
How many of your priorities have any evidence.
Evidence level
Strong, moderate or thin, based on how much independent evidence there is.
More detail

Neutral mode: skip Step 1 and Step 5; no scores or calls in Step 7.

How to use

This kit works with any AI chat that has web search turned on, for example Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity, Meta AI, Grok, DeepSeek, ChatGPT, or Claude. A local model on your own computer also works if you paste the source pages into the chat yourself.

Two ways to use it:

  • Neutral comparison: a sourced side-by-side of every choice, with no scores. Skip Steps 1 and 5.
  • Values match: every choice scored against values you supply. Run all steps, and use the score calculator for Step 5.
  1. Your profile: fill in the form. Neutral comparison needs only your election details; values match also asks for your values, in your own words. The kit works for any political view.
  2. Find your ballot: click "Copy for chat", paste it into a new chat with web search turned on, and paste the AI's answer back. The page turns it into a list of races and measures.
  3. Research each item: one new chat per race or measure. Paste each answer back; in values mode the page does the scoring math for you.
  4. Check and build: spot-check three sources, then copy the check (Step 6) and the guide (Step 7) into new chats.
  5. Your address goes only into the Find your ballot chat. Every other chat gets "Address: withheld".

Crowded races: if a race has 5 or more candidates, you can choose a focus set by a rule that is not about party; the guide still lists everyone left out and why.

Scores measure how well each choice fits your values, not who is likely to win. If you want to see viability too, ask for it in your profile (Step 1).

Your profile

Why: the AI needs to know which ballot is yours, and (if you want scores) what matters to you.

What kind of guide do you want?

You can switch later. Neutral mode never suggests a choice. Values match suggests a choice based only on the priorities you enter; the decision is always yours.

Districts can split a street, so a full address finds your exact ballot. If you prefer, enter only your city, county and state; the guide may then include races that are not on your ballot. Your address goes only into the Step 2 question, which you paste into an AI chat (that chat service may keep it). This page never saves it on this device.

The date on your ballot or your election office website. US general elections are on the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November.

Your priorities (values match only)

List 3 to 6 things you want your government to do or not do. Write them as policies, not parties or people. Each one gets a letter (A, B, C...) so the AI can tag evidence to it.

Ideas: tax level · public services · public safety · personal freedom · environment · housing · schools · clean government

If you go back and forth on an issue, list it. Evidence about it will count as neutral instead of for or against. One per line.

Specific actions that would make you not want to support someone, no matter what else they do. Only proven actions count, not accusations. A candidate with a proven deal-breaker is left out of the suggestion; their evidence is still shown. One per line.

For example your job or property. The guide notes them so you can see your own interests clearly; they do not change any score. One per line.

Scores only measure how well a choice fits your priorities. Turn this on if you also want to see polling or primary results next to each race. It never changes a score.

Races with many candidates (5 or more)

Choose a fair rule that is not about party. Everyone left out is still listed with the reason, and you can add them back. Common choices: at least 5% in the primary, or at least $10,000 raised.

        Lessons in this kit come from building and reviewing a real 2026 guide. Typical errors were: an amendment vote read as a final vote, candidates matched to the wrong seat in pamphlet text, and an uncontested race shown as contested. A review then found ways to game the score (many weak items, empty records) and web pages that try to give the AI instructions. Steps 0, 5, and 6 now guard against each of these.
        
  
        Dates, deadlines and places to vote: use your official state or county election website, or vote.org.
