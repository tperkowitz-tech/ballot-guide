Reusable · works with any AI that can search the web

Ballot Guide Prompt Kit

Build a sourced guide to your ballot. Neutral comparison by default; optional scores against your own values.

Private by design: this page collects nothing about you, and the page itself sends nothing anywhere. Read the privacy policy.

Start: Step 1, your profile

Before you start

You will need: an AI chat that can search the web (for example Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity, ChatGPT or Claude). Plan on about 5 minutes per race or measure; a ballot of 20 items takes 1 to 2 hours, so do a few at a time — progress is saved. This page writes the questions for you. You copy each question into the AI chat, then paste the AI's answer back here.

How it works

  1. Tell the page about you.
  2. Ask the AI to list your ballot.
  3. Ask the AI about each race and measure.
  4. Check a few sources and build your guide.
Words used here
Record
Something a candidate actually did (a vote, a ruling, an official act).
Funder
Who gave money to the campaign.
Questionnaire
A candidate's written answer to a published survey, such as Vote411 or Vote Smart. Counts more than a statement, less than a record.
Statement
What a candidate says, such as a website or interview.
Endorsement
Support from an issue group that publishes its criteria. All endorsements and donors on one priority count as one weak item.
Measure
A yes/no question on the ballot, such as a proposition, an initiative, or a local tax (levy).
Uncontested
Only one candidate is running.
Double-check
A second AI chat checks the first answer against its sources.
Score
A summary of the evidence found and how each piece was marked as for or against your priorities. It is not a prediction of who will win.
Range
How far unknown priorities could move the score, shown as "could be low–high". A wide range means much is unknown.
Coverage
How many of your priorities have a record, questionnaire answer or statement.
Uneven evidence
One choice has much more evidence than the other, so the call is at most a lean.
Evidence level
Strong, moderate or thin, based on how much independent evidence there is.
More detail

When to use it: once your ballot or voters' pamphlet arrives (usually 2 to 6 weeks before Election Day), when the candidate list is final and most candidate questionnaires and donor reports are out. In the last week, do a quick re-check of close races and the candidate list, since candidates can withdraw and late donor reports and news appear. Your progress stays in this browser, so you can come back.

Your profile

Why: the AI needs to know which ballot is yours, and (if you want scores) what matters to you.

What kind of guide do you want?

You can switch later. Neutral mode never suggests a choice. Values match suggests a choice based only on the priorities you enter; the decision is always yours.

Finds your exact ballot. Used only in the Step 2 question and its double-check; never saved on this device.

More about the address

Districts can split a street, so a full address finds your exact ballot. If you prefer, enter only your city, county and state; the guide may then include races that are not on your ballot. You paste the Step 2 question into an AI chat yourself, and that chat service may keep it.

The date on your ballot or your election office website.

Not sure of the date?

US general elections are on the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November. Primaries and special elections vary; your election office website lists them.

Your priorities (values match only)

List 3 to 6 things you want your government to do or not do. Write them as policies, not people. Each one gets a letter (A, B, C...) so the AI can tag evidence to it.

Ideas: tax level · public services · public safety · personal freedom · environment · housing · schools · clean government

Not sure what matters most to you? Try the Moral Foundations self-check from university researchers. The self-scoring version is a document you fill out on your own device, so nothing is sent anywhere.

If you go back and forth on an issue, list it. Evidence about it is left out of the score and listed separately. One per line.

Specific actions that would make you not want to support someone, no matter what else they do. Only proven actions count, not accusations. A candidate with a proven deal-breaker is left out of the suggestion; their evidence is still shown. One per line.

For example your job or property. The guide notes them so you can see your own interests clearly; they do not change any score. One per line.

Scores only measure how well a choice fits your priorities. Turn this on if you also want to see polling or primary results next to each race. It never changes a score.

Races with many candidates (5 or more)

Pick a fair rule, for example a minimum vote share or amount raised. Everyone left out is still listed with the reason, and you can add them back. Common choices: at least 5% in the primary, or at least $10,000 raised.

After each answer, you can ask a second AI chat to check it against its sources. It takes one more copy and paste per item and catches many mistakes. You can skip it any time.

          Privacy
          
  
          This page collects no information about you. It has no accounts, no cookies, no analytics or tracking, and no server of its own. Nothing you type is sent to this project or to anyone else by this page.
          
  
            
    
          • What stays on your computer: to keep your progress, your browser saves your profile choices, your race list and the AI answers you paste, in this browser on this device only. It stays until you press Start over or clear this site's data in your browser settings. This page never sends it anywhere.
            • 
    
          • What is never saved: your address, the AI's answer that lists your ballot, and that answer's double-check reply (both may repeat your address).
            • 
    
          • What leaves your computer, only when you do it: text you copy and paste into an AI chat. Your address goes only into the ballot-lookup question and its optional double-check. The AI service's own privacy policy covers anything you paste there. Links you open take you to other websites.
            • 
    
          • Hosting: GitHub Pages serves this page. Like any website host, GitHub receives standard information such as your IP address when the page loads. The page loads nothing from any other site: no fonts, ads or scripts. Your browser enforces this with a security policy built into the page.
            • 
    
          • Test report: the optional "Share how it went" panel only opens a GitHub form with the fields shown in its preview. Nothing is sent unless you submit that form on GitHub.
            • 
    
          • Clipboard: the page writes to your clipboard only when you press a copy button. It never reads your clipboard.
            • 
  
          
  
          Questions or a privacy problem? See the project's security and privacy policy.
          

          


          
  
          Lessons in this kit come from building and reviewing a real 2026 guide. Typical errors were: an amendment vote read as a final vote, candidates matched to the wrong seat in pamphlet text, and an uncontested race shown as contested. A review then found ways to game the score (many weak items, empty records) and web pages that try to give the AI instructions. Steps 0, 5, and 6 now guard against each of these.
